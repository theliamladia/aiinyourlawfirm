Your first automation will not work

Last week I asked you to find the places where your office types the same information twice, and to write down both systems. This week you connect them. Before you do, you should know something the tutorials leave out: your first automation will not work.

No automation works the first time. Building one is trial and error. You set up the connection you believe will work, run a test, and look at what comes out the other side. Then you test it again. What happens if a piece of information is missing? What happens when the new client is a married couple instead of a single person? What happens when a name has a hyphen, or there is no email address at all?

Each of those is a separate test, and every test runs on a pretend client in a test environment before the automation ever touches a real file. It goes live only when every version comes out right on the other side.

That is the technical half, and it is the easier half. The harder half is your staff.

Automations often take a few minutes to fire. To a busy person, a few minutes is a long time. If your staff are anything like Regina on our intake team, the first instinct is, “I can just create the Trello card myself. It doesn’t take that long.” Waiting five minutes for a sync feels slower than doing the job by hand.

So you have to convince your staff to trust the process: let the automation do its job, and move on to something else while it runs. Expect your best people to resist it, or to struggle with it at first. They are conscientious, and handing off a task they have always done themselves feels like dropping it.

Once they trust that the automations fire, the change is real. The minutes that went into building cards go to work that requires their knowledge and skill: calling clients, scheduling appointments, the front stage. Nobody hired Regina to create Trello cards.

One thing to try

Build the connection you found last week in Zapier, Make or Power Automate.

Test it with a pretend client, never a real one. Run at least three versions: a single person, a married couple, and one with a piece of information missing. Check what comes out the other side every time.

Before you turn it on, sit down with the person whose job it changes. Show them the test, tell them how long it takes to fire, and agree on what they will do with that time instead.
