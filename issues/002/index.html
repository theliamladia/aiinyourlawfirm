Where your office types the same thing twice

Last week I asked you to choose one person and one task and document it from beginning to end. This week, go back through what you documented and look for one thing: information that gets typed twice.

Double entry is one of the most common kinds of waste in a law office, and it is where I built my first automation.

Our CRM holds everything about a client file: contact information for the clients and their families, the documents, the appointments, the billing. Alongside it we use Trello, a free platform that lays our files out on a kanban board. Each file is a card and each column is a stage of the work, so anyone in the office can see at a glance which files need attention.

Early on, I sat with Regina, our intake coordinator, and watched how she opened a new file. When a new client scheduled, she entered everything into our CRM: the names, the contact details, the family, the appointment. Then she opened Trello and typed much of the same information a second time to create the card.

Every new client, twice.

Typing it twice costs more than time. Every retyped name is another chance to misspell a client’s name, and two systems that disagree about a client will eventually put the wrong version in front of someone.

The fix was a connection between the two systems. Using Zapier, I set it up so that whenever a new matter was created in our CRM, a Trello card with the same name was created automatically. Regina entered the client once, and the card appeared on its own. Zapier was the main option at the time, and we still use it today, alongside Make and Microsoft Power Automate. Most legal practice management systems, including Clio, PracticePanther and Smokeball, connect to at least one of them.

It was a purely backstage change, and it was the first automation I ever built for our firm.

Today I am building something further: an AI agent that creates and runs these connections on its own, inside our firm. The goal is to keep every one of these automations in-house, so no client information passes through an outside tool. That is more complicated, and it is not where you should start. Zapier, Make and Power Automate are reputable and very widely used, and they are the right place to begin.

Three questions for every step

Go through the process you documented, one step at a time, and ask:

Is this information already typed somewhere else? That is double entry, and it is the first thing to eliminate.

Does this step happen the same way every time? If so, it can probably run without a person.

Does this step need to happen at all? Some steps survive only because nobody ever asked why they exist.

One thing to try

Mark every place in your process where someone types information that already lives in another system. For each one, write down both systems: where the information starts, and where it gets typed again.

Then search each pair of names alongside the word “Zapier.” If both systems appear, the connection you need may already exist.

You do not have to build anything this week. Finding the double entry is the assignment.

If you did not get to last week’s assignment, start there. One person, one task, every step, nothing left out.

The latest in AI

OpenAI introduced dots, an assistant that works in the background across the apps you connect, including your email, and flags what needs your attention before you ask. While it works on its own, it can read but cannot send. Why it matters to you: email was the most common answer when we asked readers what they wish someone else could do. Dots starts at $100 a month. Before it reads a client inbox, confirm how your plan handles client data.
